# Travel and Expense Reimbursement Policy

*Document owner: Global Finance and Travel Operations. Effective date: 1 February 2025. Version: 2.6.*

## Purpose and Scope

This policy governs business travel and the reimbursement of expenses incurred while travelling on company business. It applies to all employees, contractors, and interns who travel at company expense, and to anyone who approves or processes a travel claim. The objective is to keep travel safe, practical, and cost-effective while giving employees clear limits rather than case-by-case negotiation. All travel must have a documented business purpose and the approval of the employee's manager before booking. Employees must register their trip in the travel portal, check the destination's entry requirements and security guidance before departure, and report any incident abroad to travel@company.example the same day. Finance audits a sample of expense reports every quarter.

## Booking Travel and the Corporate Card

All airfare and hotel bookings must be made through the corporate travel portal, and the corporate card is mandatory for airfare and hotel bookings. Employees who book outside the portal without written approval will not be reimbursed and may be held personally liable for cancellation fees. Bookings should be made at least 14 days before departure to obtain reasonable fares; where a trip is confirmed later, the reason must be recorded in the expense report. The corporate card must never be used for personal expenses. Personal cards may be used for meals and ground transport where the corporate card is not accepted, and are reimbursed through the normal expense process.

## Air Travel

Domestic flights are booked in economy class only. Business class is permitted only for flights longer than 6 hours and requires VP approval, requested through the travel portal before booking. Premium economy may be booked for flights longer than 6 hours with manager approval when it is cheaper than a flexible economy fare. Employees should choose the lowest logical fare within four hours of the preferred departure time and should not split tickets across carriers on the same journey. Seat selection and one checked bag may be reimbursed where the fare does not include them; lounge access, priority boarding, and upgrades are personal expenses. Flight changes caused by the company are fully reimbursable.

## Accommodation

Hotel costs are capped at USD 180 per night domestic and USD 250 per night international in tier-1 cities. Where the negotiated rate at a conference hotel exceeds the cap, the employee may book the conference hotel with manager approval recorded in the trip request. Employees should stay in a standard room, book refundable rates where the itinerary may change, and cancel unused nights before the cancellation deadline. Room service, minibar, movies, and spa charges are personal expenses. Where two employees travel together, each books a separate room unless a shared room is explicitly agreed in advance. Stays beyond five nights require director approval and a cost comparison against a serviced apartment.

## Meals and Incidentals

The meal allowance is USD 60 per day domestic and USD 80 per day international, covering breakfast, lunch, dinner, and incidentals. Employees do not need to submit individual meal receipts within the allowance, but must record the daily total in the expense report. Where a meal is provided by the company, a conference, or included in the hotel rate, the allowance is reduced accordingly. Alcohol is not reimbursable and must be excluded from every claim. Employees travelling with customers may exceed the daily allowance for a hosted meal with manager approval and an itemised receipt listing attendees and the business purpose.

## Ground Transportation

Ground transport is reimbursed for rideshare or taxi up to USD 40 per ride. A ride above that limit requires a written justification in the expense report and manager approval, which is normally granted only for airport transfers outside public transport hours or for safety reasons. Employees should use public transport where it is safe, reliable, and practical, and should use the standard class rather than premium options. Rental cars require manager approval and must be booked in the compact category unless equipment or team size requires a larger vehicle. Parking, tolls, and fuel for rental cars are reimbursable with receipts. Traffic fines and parking tickets are personal expenses.

## Approval Thresholds

Every trip needs a trip request approved by the employee's manager before booking. Expenses above USD 1,000 require director approval, and expenses above USD 5,000 require VP approval. The thresholds apply to the total value of a single expense report as well as to any single line item, so a trip that totals more than the threshold cannot be split into smaller claims to avoid review. Approvers must verify that the expense is legitimate, within policy, and supported by receipts before approving. An approver may not approve their own expenses or those of a person in their reporting chain; in that case the next level of management approves.

## Expense Submission and Receipts

Expense reports must be submitted within 30 days of the trip ending, and itemised receipts are required for any single expense above USD 25. Receipts must show the vendor, date, amount, currency, and items purchased; card statements alone are not sufficient. Claims in a foreign currency are converted at the corporate card rate on the transaction date. Reports submitted after 30 days may be declined, and any report submitted more than 60 days after the trip ends will be declined unless the delay was caused by the company. Employees must repay personal expenses charged to the corporate card within 30 days of the card statement. Finance processes approved reports within ten business days.

## Non-Reimbursable Expenses

The following items are never reimbursable: alcohol; personal extensions of a business trip, including the extra hotel nights, meals, and changed flights; spa treatments, gym access, and hotel entertainment; minibar and in-room movies; airline lounge memberships and seat upgrades; traffic fines and parking tickets; lost or stolen personal property; clothing and toiletries other than emergency replacements approved by a manager; and any expense incurred by a spouse, partner, or family member. Personal extensions of a business trip are not reimbursable even where the employee pays the fare difference. Employees who combine business travel with personal leave must book the personal portion separately, and the company reimburses only the cost of the business itinerary.
